# The storage model

[Scientific overview](README.md) · [Radiative-rank law](rank-law.md) · [Encoding and precision](encoding-and-precision.md) · [Limitations](limitations.md)

The task is to store an unknown quantum register in $`N`$ two-level emitters while they remain coupled to a vacuum bosonic environment. Encoding and recovery occur at the two endpoints. During storage, the only quantum carriers containing the logical input are the emitters themselves.

The central resource is the number of independent **spatial coupling profiles**. It is different from the number of reservoir oscillators: many frequencies or cavity modes may couple through the same spatial profiles.

## 1. Emitters and supplied coupling information

For emitter $`j`$, write

```math
\sigma_j^-=|0\rangle\langle1|_j,
\qquad n_j=|1\rangle\langle1|_j,
\qquad \mathsf N=\sum_{j=1}^{N}n_j.
```

The supplied profile matrix is $`G\in\mathbb C^{r\times N}`$, of row rank $`r`$, with $`0\le r\le N`$. Its collective lowering operators and common dark space are

```math
L_a=\sum_{j=1}^{N}G_{aj}\sigma_j^-\quad(a=1,\ldots,r),
\qquad
\mathcal K_G=\bigcap_{a=1}^{r}\ker L_a.
```

For $`r=0`$, the intersection is the whole emitter Hilbert space. An invertible change of basis among the rows of $`G`$ changes the list of operators but leaves $`\mathcal K_G`$ unchanged. The physically relevant datum is therefore their row span.

The profile coefficients may be complex, unequal, disordered, or zero. The dimension theorem imposes no minimum spacing between coefficients and no permutation symmetry. The coefficients are known and fixed during storage, or change only by mixing within their supplied row span. A profile outside that span is an additional noise direction and changes the model.

The dimension theorem is an existence statement for arbitrary supplied coefficients. The [compiler account](encoding-and-precision.md) separately specifies its finite input representation, coefficient precision, and gate costs. Knowing a profile mathematically does not include a cost-free experimental calibration procedure.

## 2. A full vacuum light–matter Hamiltonian

Let $`a_\lambda`$ denote bosonic annihilation operators, with the reservoir initially in its joint vacuum $`|\mathrm{vac}\rangle`$. Begin with any finite number of modes; their number is not bounded by $`r`$. Take

```math
H_A=\omega\mathsf N,
\qquad
H_B|\mathrm{vac}\rangle=E_B|\mathrm{vac}\rangle,
```

and an excitation-conserving rotating-wave interaction

```math
H_{\mathrm{int}}(t)
=\sum_{\lambda,a}
\left[
\eta_{\lambda a}(t)a_\lambda^\dagger L_a
+\overline{\eta_{\lambda a}(t)}L_a^\dagger a_\lambda
\right].
```

The free reservoir dynamics preserve the vacuum. A standard example is $`H_B=\sum_\lambda\nu_\lambda a_\lambda^\dagger a_\lambda`$; number-preserving mixing of reservoir modes is also allowed. Coefficients may depend on time, provided the resulting propagator is well defined. For a number-preserving $`H_B`$ with finitely many modes, excitation conservation restricts the initially accessible dynamics to a finite total-excitation sector. More generally, the theorem only needs a vacuum eigenstate and a well-defined propagator on the stated sector. The same invariant-subspace argument applies to an infinite reservoir under these domain assumptions.

The coupling of mode $`\lambda`$ to emitter $`j`$ is

```math
g_{\lambda j}(t)=\sum_{a=1}^{r}\eta_{\lambda a}(t)G_{aj}.
```

Thus arbitrary spectral structure, mode frequencies, coupling strengths, and reservoir memory are compatible with the model, as long as these spatial vectors remain in the supplied span. This is a restriction on the interaction; it is not a claim that a generic multimode reservoir has low spatial rank.

All emitters have the same transition frequency $`\omega`$. Additional terms of the form

```math
H_{\mathrm{dark}}(t)
=\sum_{a,b}h_{ab}(t)L_a^\dagger L_b,
\qquad h(t)=h(t)^\dagger,
```

may be added: they annihilate $`\mathcal K_G`$. A general exchange Hamiltonian or unequal local transition frequencies need not have this property. Their effects are outside the exact theorem unless their action on the chosen code is established separately.

## 3. Exact vacuum invariance

**Proposition 1.** For every $`|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal K_G`$,

```math
H_{\mathrm{int}}(t)
\bigl(|\psi\rangle\otimes|\mathrm{vac}\rangle\bigr)=0.
```

Moreover, $`\mathcal K_G`$ is invariant under $`\mathsf N`$. Consequently the full evolution generated by $`H_A+H_B+H_{\mathrm{dark}}(t)+H_{\mathrm{int}}(t)`$ satisfies

```math
U(t)\bigl(|\psi\rangle\otimes|\mathrm{vac}\rangle\bigr)
=e^{-iE_Bt}
\bigl(e^{-i\omega t\mathsf N}|\psi\rangle\bigr)
\otimes|\mathrm{vac}\rangle.
```

The bath remains exactly in its initial vacuum and acquires no information about the logical input.

**Proof.** The term $`a_\lambda^\dagger L_a`$ vanishes because $`L_a|\psi\rangle=0`$. The term $`L_a^\dagger a_\lambda`$ vanishes because $`a_\lambda|\mathrm{vac}\rangle=0`$. Also

```math
[\mathsf N,L_a]=-L_a,
```

so emitter excitation-number evolution preserves the common kernel. Finally $`L_a^\dagger L_b|\psi\rangle=0`$ for every pair of indices. These facts identify an invariant subspace on which the Hamiltonian reduces to $`\omega\mathsf N+E_B`$. This proves the formula, including for time-dependent interaction strengths. $`\square`$

The argument uses the full Hamiltonian. It invokes neither a Markov approximation nor weak coupling, rapid bath relaxation, or repeated bath resets. It does assume the vacuum and the rotating-wave interaction. Thermal occupation or counterrotating terms generally invalidate the annihilation argument.

## 4. Logical input, reference, and recovery

Let $`Q`$ be a logical system of dimension $`D`$, and choose an isometry

```math
V:\mathbb C^{D}\longrightarrow\mathcal K_G.
```

The input $`\rho_{QR}`$ may be arbitrary and entangled with an inaccessible reference $`R`$. The initial physical state is

```math
(V\otimes I_R)\rho_{QR}(V^\dagger\otimes I_R)
\otimes|\mathrm{vac}\rangle\langle\mathrm{vac}|.
```

Proposition 1 holds on the entire code and therefore on every such reference-entangled state. At a known duration $`t`$, recovery first reverses the known emitter evolution $`e^{-i\omega t\mathsf N}`$ and then applies the inverse isometry on the code. Its extension outside the code may be any trace-preserving quantum operation. The resulting logical channel is exactly the identity:

```math
\mathcal R_t\circ\Phi_t\circ\mathcal V
=\mathrm{id}_Q,
\qquad
\frac12\left\|
\mathcal R_t\circ\Phi_t\circ\mathcal V
-\mathrm{id}_Q
\right\|_\diamond=0.
```

Here $`\Phi_t`$ is the reduced emitter channel and $`\mathcal V(\rho)=V\rho V^\dagger`$. The diamond statement includes all reference systems; it is not only a survival-probability or basis-state assertion.

A single excitation sector removes the timing correction. Write $`\mathcal H_k=\ker(\mathsf N-kI)`$ and $`\mathcal K_{G,k}=\mathcal K_G\cap\mathcal H_k`$. If the image of $`V`$ lies in $`\mathcal K_{G,k}`$, its evolution is one global phase. A fixed decoder then works for every storage duration. The [rank law](rank-law.md#3-a-stationary-code) bounds the extra dimension cost of this stationary choice.

## 5. Markovian counterpart

The same code is dark for the time-dependent Lindblad generator

```math
\mathcal L_t(\rho)
=-i[\omega\mathsf N+H_{\mathrm{dark}}(t),\rho]
+\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}(t)
\left(
L_a\rho L_b^\dagger
-\frac12\{L_b^\dagger L_a,\rho\}
\right),
\qquad C(t)\succeq0.
```

For a density operator supported on $`\mathcal K_G`$, every dissipative term vanishes. Its evolution is precisely $`\rho\mapsto e^{-i\omega t\mathsf N}\rho e^{i\omega t\mathsf N}`$. Positivity of $`C(t)`$ makes this a legitimate collective-loss model; its eigenvalues and time variation do not affect the dark-code dimension.

This is a counterpart of the Hamiltonian theorem, rather than an approximation required by its proof. If a larger supplied operator span is used than the active dissipator actually needs, the resulting code may be conservative but remains valid.

## 6. Resources and scope

Storage is passive after encoding. It uses no measurements, feedback, resets, dynamical decoupling, postselection, fresh pure carriers, or helper quantum register during the storage interval. The initial logical state is transferred into the emitters; retaining another copy or keeping the original register outside the noise is not part of the protocol. The vacuum is an explicit environmental assumption.

Endpoint operations may jointly address emitters. Their ideal existence, efficient compilation, finite gate accuracy, and physical calibration are distinct questions. The [encoding account](encoding-and-precision.md) gives a polynomial endpoint construction in a specified rank regime; the general sublinear-rank existence theorem does not claim an efficient encoder for every profile sequence.

The result concerns this vacuum interaction class and its common-kernel codes. It does not establish storage against arbitrary thermal noise, independent emission, profile drift outside the known span, counterrotating light–matter coupling, or a generic free-space exchange Hamiltonian. It is not an unrestricted quantum-capacity or fault-tolerance theorem.

The dark-space annihilation criterion and its passive-memory interpretation are established decoherence-free-subspace theory. The [related-work comparison](../references/related-work.md) credits that foundation and distinguishes the arbitrary-profile radiative-rank law from earlier symmetric and single-mode constructions.
